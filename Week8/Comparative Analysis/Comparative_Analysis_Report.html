Comparative Analysis (GardenSewa)¶

1. Introduction¶

This report analyzes a curated dataset of 93 garden and landscaping service listings gathered from 25 companies operating across Kathmandu, Lalitpur, and Bhaktapur. Each listing captures the service offered, the company name, the type of provider (organized company, individual/informal, or app/marketplace platform), contact details, location served, and the source links.

The goal of this analysis is to understand:

  • Which garden services are most commonly offered in the market
  • How the market is structured by provider type (formal vs. informal)
  • How service availability is distributed geographically
  • Which companies dominate the market in terms of service breadth

The following sections walk through the dataset, present six visualizations, and close with a consolidated set of key findings.

5. Key Findings¶

  1. Market is service-concentrated: Pest and Disease Control is the single most listed service (18 of 93, approximately 19%), while four services, Soil Improvement, Plant Propagation, Rooftop Gardening, and Artificial Gardening, have only one provider each. This signals both a demand hotspot and several underserved niches.

  2. Formal businesses dominate: Organized companies make up 89.2% of all listings, versus just 5.4% each for individual/informal providers and app/marketplace platforms. The gardening services sector in this dataset is not yet a gig-economy-driven market.

  3. App-based platforms are narrowly focused: Where marketplace platforms do appear, they cluster around only five high-frequency services, such as Lawn Care, Pest and Disease Control, and Garden Maintenance, rather than the full 20-service catalog. This suggests an opportunity for platforms to expand into niche categories.

  4. Kathmandu dominates geographically: With 79.6% of all listings, Kathmandu is the clear center of the market. Lalitpur (15.1%) and especially Bhaktapur (5.4%) show far lower provider density, and Bhaktapur's coverage is limited to just 3 of the 20 services.

  5. Market leadership is concentrated among a few companies: Ajima Nursery and KTM Nursery together account for 23 of 93 listings (approximately 25%), while the top 4 companies account for approximately 44% of all listings. Meanwhile, 12 of the 25 companies (48%) appear only once, reflecting a long tail of small, single-service providers.

  6. Geographic service gaps exist: Bhaktapur has no presence in the two highest-demand categories, Pest and Disease Control and Garden Maintenance, representing a clear expansion opportunity for providers willing to serve that area.

Overall Takeaway¶

The Kathmandu Valley garden services market is dominated by a small number of established, multi-service companies concentrated in Kathmandu, with pest control and general maintenance serving as the anchor services. Niche services, informal/gig providers, and areas outside Kathmandu, particularly Bhaktapur, remain comparatively underserved, pointing to potential areas for market growth or new entrants.
